> USC FIT (USC's First Fitness Matching Platform) was the team final for **CSCI 201 — Principles of Software Development**: a full-stack Java web application for USC students to find and join campus fitness activities.

## The problem

Working out is easier with people, but finding a partner at the right facility, time, and skill level is friction nobody wants to manage in group chats. USC FIT turns that into a structured matching problem: students post and discover activities, filter by interest and skill, and commit to a scheduled session — with light accountability so plans actually hold.

## What it does

- **USC-email registration and login** — accounts are validated against a `usc.edu` address; guests can browse activities before signing up.
- **Event creation and scheduling** — students create activities tied to a facility and time, and others join them.
- **Profiles with interests and skill levels** — so matches are between people who actually fit, not just whoever's free.
- **Facility information and ratings** — campus gyms and courts carry details and a rating, surfacing where to go.
- **Cancellation penalties** — a small accountability mechanic that discourages flaking and keeps scheduled sessions real.

## How it's built

A classic server-rendered MVC stack, built deliberately without a heavyweight framework so the request lifecycle stays visible end to end:

- **Java Servlets + JSP** handle routing, request handling, and views.
- **MySQL over JDBC** is the system of record; the schema (`schema.sql`) models users, activities, facilities, and sign-ups.
- **Maven** builds the project into a WAR that deploys to **Tomcat**.

The interesting engineering is in the boundaries — validating USC identity at the auth edge, keeping the activity/sign-up data model consistent under concurrent joins, and enforcing the cancellation-penalty rules in one place rather than scattering them across handlers.

## Context

Built as a team project for CSCI 201. The codebase lives on GitHub; the README documents the feature set, the MySQL schema setup, and the Maven → Tomcat deploy path.
